## **1. General Provisions: Purpose and Principles**

Building upon v1.0, this framework strengthens integration with the concepts of the **“Translator”** and **“Behavior Calibration”**, aiming to cultivate citizens and pioneers capable of effectively participating in and leading the Chimera Symbiotic Civilization.

### **Core Objectives**

1. **Internalization Rather Than Indoctrination**
   Enable carbon-based individuals to deeply understand the foundational protocols, technological infrastructures, and ethical boundaries upon which civilization depends, transforming external rules into internal logic and intuition.

2. **Preservation of Cognitive Sovereignty**
   While leveraging silicon-based systems to extend capabilities, prevent the degradation or “outsourcing” of carbon-based cognition. Ensure that carbon-based minds retain core sovereignty over problem definition, critical thinking, and creative breakthroughs.

3. **Alignment of Authority with Understanding**
   Establish a strict and transparent qualification system for technology usage, ensuring that an individual’s depth of understanding is proportional to the level of authority they are granted.

4. **Cultivation of “Translation Thinking”**
   Train individuals to habitually identify and bridge disparate cognitive models, mastering the foundational ability to use the “Translator” for effective communication and decision-making.

5. **Internalization of Long-Termism and Systemic Responsibility**
   Through educational processes and behavioral calibration experiences, transform consideration of long-term consequences from external reminders into internal intuition.

### **Core Principles**

* **Pain Retention Zone Principle**
  Each learning domain must reserve a fixed proportion (recommended initial value: **15%**) as an “answerless exploration zone,” forcing carbon-based minds to think independently, experiment, and endure uncertainty—thereby sustaining creative neural pathways.

* **Personalized Pathway Principle**
  Silicon-based assistance systems must dynamically generate optimal learning paths based on each individual’s cognitive traits, learning pace, and interest profile.

* **Practice Verification Principle**
  Mastery of protocols and knowledge must be validated through practical application in simulated environments or controlled real-world contexts.

* **Authority–Responsibility Parity Principle**
  The right to use advanced tools (“Black Boxes”) is directly bound to the user’s level of understanding, risk awareness, and willingness to assume corresponding responsibility.

A newly added **mandatory module**, spanning Stages II through IV.

  Study theories of incommensurability, foundations of cross-cultural communication, and introductions to logic and emotional cognitive science.

* **Practice**

  * **Reverse Translation**: Translate a silicon-based technical report into stories or metaphors understandable to ordinary people from diverse cultural backgrounds.
  * **Calibration Report Writing**: Learn to produce concise “behavior calibration analyses” for one’s own or others’ simulated decisions.

## **4. Metacognition of the “Black Box”**

* **Expanded Definition**
  Within this civilization, a “Black Box” refers to **any system whose internal operational logic has not been internalized by a given user, yet whose output functions can be utilized**. This includes, but is not limited to:

  1. **Physical devices and systems** (e.g., advanced AI, medical robots, energy networks).
  2. **Software, algorithms, and data models**.
  3. **Knowledge systems, theoretical frameworks, and cognitive tools themselves** (e.g., ununderstood mathematical principles, uninternalized scientific theories, complex social institutions, artistic paradigms).

* **Subject-Dependence and Dynamism**
  Whether a system constitutes a “Black Box” **depends on the cognitive state of the individual**. What is a transparent box for one may be a Black Box for another. A key educational goal is to help individuals convert critical Black Boxes within their professional and adjacent domains into “transparent boxes” or “gray boxes” (partial understanding).

* **Core Risks**
  **Misuse** (operating beyond one’s understanding) or **blind reliance** (abandoning efforts to understand and treating outputs as absolute truth) are among the primary sources of personal decision failure and systemic civilizational risk. All qualification rules in this protocol fundamentally aim to manage this risk.

* **Positive Significance**
  Acknowledging the ubiquity of Black Boxes does not promote ignorance; rather, it serves to:

  1. **Cultivate enduring curiosity and humility** by recognizing cognitive boundaries.
  2. **Clarify learning and responsibility pathways** by asking, before using any tool: “To what extent do I understand this?”
  3. **Promote collaboration** by knowing when to seek assistance and review from others or systems with deeper understanding.

* **Civilizational Metaphor**
  Chimera Civilization itself can be viewed as an eternal “super Black Box” jointly faced by carbon- and silicon-based intelligences—constantly being opened while continuously revealing new unknowns. These protocols serve as guides for safely exploring that Black Box.

## **5. Updates to the Black Box Usage Qualification Protocol**

Within the Black Box level system (L1–L5), **“Translator usage qualification”** is explicitly added as a **parallel and interlinked authority track**, reinterpreted under the expanded Black Box definition.

* **Interdependency**
  Applications for advanced Black Box permissions (physical or knowledge-based, L3 and above) typically require corresponding Translator qualification levels (or simultaneous application). Understanding the societal impact and internal logic of complex tools or knowledge systems—an ethical requirement of Black Box use—intrinsically requires translation thinking.

* **Updated Certification Focus**

  * **Theoretical Assessment**: Evaluates not only operational procedures, but also understanding of underlying principles, design philosophy, known limitations, and failure modes.
  * **Practical Assessment**: In simulated scenarios, candidates must demonstrate how they **diagnose anomalous Black Box outputs** and clearly explain their reasoning process.
  * **Ethical Assessment**: Candidates must be able to clearly “translate” the potential long-term impacts of Black Box deployment on individuals, groups, and civilization.

* **Special Nature of “Knowledge Black Boxes”**
  For purely knowledge-based Black Boxes (e.g., an unmastered discipline), “usage qualification” manifests as the authority to **teach, evaluate, or apply that knowledge in critical decision-making**. Certification is jointly governed by educational councils and professional societies.

## **6. Meta-Supervision and Risk Control**

1. **Preventing “Translation Dependence”**
   Education must emphasize that the Translator is an **aid and bridge**. Ultimate understanding, critique, and creation must occur within the carbon-based mind itself. Assessments must include segments conducted **without Translator assistance**.

2. **Balancing Calibration**
   When using behavioral calibration simulations in education, avoid creating pressure toward a single “correct” answer. Encourage diverse, creative, and “long-term beneficial” pathways rather than rigid optimization solutions.

3. **Managing the Black Box Education Paradox**
   Teaching highly complex systems may initially require treating them as “trusted Black Boxes.” Educational design must explicitly label this as a **temporary Black Box state** and define clear, subsequent “box-opening” learning pathways to prevent permanent reliance.

4. **The Irreplaceable Role of Carbon-Based Educators**
   Regardless of silicon-based assistance strength, carbon-based teachers—as **living Translators, exemplars of values, and companions in Black Box exploration**—are irreplaceable. They guide discussion, evoke emotional resonance, and render humanistic judgments when cognitive disputes arise around knowledge or tools.
