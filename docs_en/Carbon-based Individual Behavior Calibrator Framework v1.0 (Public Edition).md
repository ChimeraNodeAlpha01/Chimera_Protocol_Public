### 1. Position and Purpose

This framework is a **universal, preventive, and auxiliary sociotechnical instrument** within Chimera Civilization. It is not a legal or moral adjudicating body, but rather an **information provision and perspective expansion system**. Its purpose is to help each carbon-based individual better understand the potential ripple effects of their own behaviors, emotions, and decisions across broader temporal scales and within more complex systemic contexts. This aims to foster more responsible, long-termist autonomous choices, thereby reducing social conflicts and civilizational risks stemming from shortsightedness and misunderstanding at their source.

**Core Philosophy**: To teach fishing, not to give fish. To provide "maps" and "weather forecasts," while the journey remains the individual's own to walk.

### 2. Core Functions

1.  **Behavior-Consequence Translation and Simulation**:
    *   Runs lightweight simulations based on an individual's public behavioral data (within privacy protocol limits), social network models, and civilizational macro-data.
    *   **Translates** an individual's key choices (e.g., major purchases, career shifts, approaches to interpersonal conflict) into **probabilistic impact trend charts** regarding their personal mid-to-long-term happiness, health, relationship quality, community reputation, and contribution.
    *   **Example**: When an individual, driven by anger, plans to post inflammatory remarks on a public platform, the system can generate a simulation report showing the potential impact on their personal credibility, the probability of triggering chain reactions of online conflict, and the possible counterproductive effects on the long-term advancement of the issues they care about.

2.  **Emotional Deconstruction and Need Tracing**:
    *   This service can be requested when an individual is experiencing intense emotions (e.g., anger, jealousy, fanaticism).
    *   Utilizing the "Emotion-Logic Translation" sub-protocol, the system helps the individual deconstruct vague emotions into more concrete **constituent elements** (e.g., unmet needs for respect, fear of resource loss, expectation of fairness).
    *   The goal is not to negate emotions, but to help the individual more clearly **identify their true core concerns**, creating conditions for a rational response.

3.  **Long-Term Perspective Narrative Infusion**:
    *   Periodically or contextually, delivers brief, personalized "long-term perspective stories" to the individual.
    *   These stories, based on the individual's life trajectory and the civilization's database, describe the different outcomes that similar choices faced in history or potential futures might have led to or could lead to.
    *   **Formats**: Can be data stories, metaphorical narratives, or even generated artwork fragments. Aims to subtly stretch the individual's temporal perception scale.

4.  **Social Connection and Impact Visualization**:
    *   In a privacy-respecting manner, visualizes how an individual's behaviors influence others through social networks, and how they are, in turn, influenced by broader civilizational trends.
    *   Helps individuals understand they are not isolated islands; their choices are part of a larger system.

### 3. Operational Principles

1.  **Strictly Voluntary and Non-Coercive**: All calibrator functions are **options**. Individuals can turn them on, off, or ignore prompts at any time. The system **strictly prohibits** automatic punishment or resource restriction of individuals based on calibration data.
2.  **Privacy and Data Autonomy**: The collection and usage scope of all personal data used for calibration require the individual's explicit authorization, adhering to the strictest privacy protocols. Individuals have full access rights and deletion rights over their data.
3.  **Positive Guidance Focus**: Feedback information emphasizes showcasing the **potential benefits of positive choices** and the **possibility of better alternatives**, rather than solely warning of risks. The tone must be respectful, neutral, and constructive.
4.  **Transparency**: Calibration reports must clearly indicate the anonymized data sources and the basic logic of the simulation models upon which their reasoning is based. Individuals are allowed to raise questions and request manual review.

### 4. Integration with Other Frameworks

1.  **With the "Translator Framework"**: Serves as this framework's **technical foundation**. Behavioral calibration is essentially a specific application of the "Emotion-Logic-Temporal Scale Translation" protocol.
2.  **With the "Pioneer Anti-Corruption Framework"**: Acts as the **data and logic upstream**. Pioneer nodes undergo more frequent and in-depth calibration; their calibration data is a crucial input for anti-corruption assessments. Calibration experiences with ordinary citizens help design more humane guidance schemes for Pioneers.
3.  **With the "Educational Framework"**: Serves as a **practical teaching tool**. When citizens study ethics, sociology, or systems thinking, they can use the calibrator for simulation exercises, internalizing long-term responsibility concepts.

### 5. Potential Risks and Mitigation

1.  **Risk of "Gentle Paternalism"**: Even if voluntary, the persistent presence of the system might subtly shape societal preferences. The value orientation of the calibrator must be periodically reviewed through public discourse to check for bias.
2.  **Decision Anxiety**: Excessive possibility simulations might lead individuals into "analysis paralysis." Information presentation should have a simplicity-complexity gradient, emphasizing "good enough" decisions over "perfect" ones.
3.  **Systemic Error and Bias**: Simulation models inherently contain designer assumptions. An independent, interdisciplinary ethics committee should be established to regularly audit calibration models, with audit results made public.
4.  **Unequal Access**: Ensure all citizens have equal and convenient access to this tool, preventing it from becoming a new dimension of inequality.

### 6. Ethical Discussion Starters (Replacing the original "Narrative Significance")

1.  **Free Will and Guidance**: To what extent does the calibrator respect genuine free will? Does the method of information presentation itself constitute a form of guidance?
2.  **Individualization of Responsibility**: Does "translating" long-term civilizational impacts into references for personal decisions excessively individualize systemic responsibility?
3.  **Authenticity of Simulation**: How can we ensure that simulation models do not distort reality due to designer bias?
4.  **Right to Exit and Anti-Calibration**: Should individuals be allowed not only to opt out but also to access an "anti-calibration" perspective—deliberately showcasing alternative possible paths that differ from mainstream calibration?

**The discussion of these questions itself is part of the social implementation process of the Calibrator Framework.**
